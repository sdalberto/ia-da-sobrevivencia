# Water: finding, purifying and storing it

Contaminated water kills more people in disasters than lack of food. All water from an unknown source should be considered **unsafe to drink** until it has been treated.

## How much you need

- **Minimum for drinking:** 2.5 to 3 liters per person per day (more in heat, during exertion, for pregnant or breastfeeding women and for the sick).
- **Total basic need** (drinking, cooking, hygiene): 7.5 to 15 liters per person per day in an emergency.
- Pets: about 50–60 mL per kg of body weight per day.

> Source: The Sphere Handbook (2018), minimum standards for water supply in humanitarian response. https://spherestandards.org/handbook/

## Where to find water

- **Household reservoirs:** water tank, water heater (boiler), pipes (open the lowest tap in the house), toilet cistern (the tank, not the bowl).
- **Rain:** the cleanest source available in most places. Use tarps, roofs and gutters; discard the first minutes of rain, which wash dirt off the roof.
- **Rivers, lakes, springs:** prefer running water; collect upstream of villages and animals.
- **Dew:** wipe a clean cloth over vegetation before dawn and wring it out.
- **Plant transpiration:** a clear plastic bag tied around a leafy branch in the sun; water condenses at the bottom.
- **Shallow wells:** dig in low, damp spots, on the outside of bends in dry riverbeds.

**Avoid:** water near dumps, industries, mines, areas flooded by sewage or floodwater (floods carry sewage and chemicals). Seawater or urine must **not** be drunk without distillation.

## Step 1 — clarify (remove visible dirt)

Cloudy water shields microorganisms from chlorine and sunlight. Before disinfecting:

1. Let it stand for a few hours and take only the top part.
2. Filter it through a clean cloth folded several times (cotton T-shirt, coffee filter).
3. **Improvised layered filter** (cut PET bottle, from top to bottom): cloth → fine sand → crushed charcoal (do not use barbecue charcoal with "quick-light" additives) → coarse sand → gravel → cloth.

> ⚠️ An improvised sand and charcoal filter **improves appearance and smell, but does NOT make water safe**. Always disinfect afterwards.

## Step 2 — disinfect

### Boiling (most reliable method)

- Bring the water to a **rolling boil (bubbling) for 1 minute**.
- Above 2,000 m altitude, boil for **3 minutes**.
- Let it cool covered. To improve the taste, pour it back and forth between containers a few times (adds oxygen).

> Source: CDC — Making Water Safe in an Emergency. https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/index.html

### Household bleach / hypochlorite (chlorine)

Use only bleach that is **unscented, without color-safe bleach and without additives**, within its expiry date (chlorine loses strength with time and heat).

| Product | Dose per liter of clear water | Waiting time |
|---|---|---|
| Sodium hypochlorite 2.5% (bottle distributed free by Brazil's public health system, SUS) | 2 drops | 30 minutes |
| Common household bleach 2.0–2.5% ("água sanitária" in Brazil) | 2 drops | 30 minutes |
| Bleach 6% (US standard) | 2 drops (8 drops per gallon) | 30 minutes |
| Bleach 8.25% | 1–2 drops (6 drops per gallon) | 30 minutes |

- Cloudy or very cold water: **double the dose** and wait longer.
- After 30 minutes there should be a **slight smell of chlorine**. If there isn't, repeat the dose and wait another 15 minutes.

> Sources: Ministério da Saúde (Brazilian Ministry of Health) — guidance on using 2.5% sodium hypochlorite in water for human consumption; CDC — Emergency Disinfection of Drinking Water. https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water

### SODIS — solar disinfection

1. Use **clear PET bottles, label removed, up to 2 liters**, without heavy scratches.
2. The water must be clear (you can read large letters through the full bottle).
3. Lay the bottles down in full sun, preferably on a roof tile or metal sheet.
4. **6 hours** of strong sun; **2 consecutive days** if the sky is more than half cloudy.
5. Does not work well in continuous rain.

> Source: Eawag/SANDEC — SODIS Manual. https://www.sodis.ch

### Commercial filters and tablets

- Ceramic or membrane filters (0.1–0.2 micron) remove bacteria and protozoa; most **do not remove viruses**. Combine with chlorine when the water comes from areas with sewage.
- Sodium dichloroisocyanurate (NaDCC) or chlorine dioxide tablets: follow the package instructions.
- Household clay (candle) filters improve water but do not replace boiling/chlorine for high-risk water.

## Distillation (salt water, chemically contaminated water)

Distillation removes salt, metals and most contaminants:

- **Pot + upside-down lid:** boil water in a pot; place the lid upside down with a cup floating/resting in the center. Steam condenses on the lid and drips into the cup. Low yield, but it works.
- **Solar still:** dig a hole in the sun, place a container in the center, cover with clear plastic sealed at the edges with soil and a small stone in the center of the plastic, above the container. You can put vegetation or dirty water in the hole. Yields about 0.5–1 L per day.

## Storage

- Food-grade containers, clean and with lids; never reuse chemical containers.
- Store in a dark, cool place. Replace stored water every 6 months or treat it again.
- For every 1,000 liters of cistern water, the common maintenance dose is about 100 mL of 2.5% bleach, waiting 30 minutes (follow local health authority guidance).
- Draw water with a ladle or tap, without dipping hands or mugs in.

## Rainwater harvesting

- 1 mm of rain on 1 m² of roof = 1 liter of water.
- Example: 50 m² roof with 20 mm of rain → up to 1,000 L (about 20% is lost).
- Use a first-flush diverter, mosquito screen at the inlet and a lid on the cistern (dengue).
- Even rainwater should be disinfected before drinking.

## Signs of dehydration

Thirst, dark and scanty urine, dry mouth, dizziness, headache, fatigue. In children: crying without tears, sunken fontanelle (soft spot), dry diapers for more than 6 hours. See oral rehydration in the [First aid](07-first-aid-medicine.md) guide.
