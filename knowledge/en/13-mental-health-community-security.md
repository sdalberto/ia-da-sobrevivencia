# Mental health, community and security

## The mind is the main piece of equipment

In long crises, despair, panic and conflict kill as much as hunger. Survivors usually have this in common: **calm, routine, purpose and bonds**.

### The STOP technique (when panic sets in)

- **S**it down (stop).
- **T**hink and calm down (slow breathing: inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4, breathe out for 6).
- **O**bserve (what is around me, what I have, what I need).
- **P**lan (one simple, concrete next step).

### Routine and purpose

- Fixed times to wake up, eat, work, rest and sleep.
- Tasks for everyone, including children and elderly people (everyone feels useful).
- Small daily goals and celebration when they are achieved.
- Leisure moments: music, stories, games, reading, prayer or meditation according to each person's beliefs.
- Sleep: 7–9 hours for adults whenever possible; take turns on watch.

### Children

- Keep the routine and explain the situation in simple, truthful words.
- Play and drawing help process fear.
- Give small responsibilities (watering the garden, caring for the animals).

### Grief and trauma

- Let people talk (or stay silent) without forcing them.
- **Psychological first aid:** look, listen and link — offer comfort, help with basic needs and connect the person with support from family and community.
- Watch for warning signs: talk of giving up on life, total isolation, stopping eating or taking care of oneself. Stay close, don't leave the person alone and seek support. In Brazil, CVV answers at 188; in the US, call or text 988 (when phones are working).

> Source: WHO — *Psychological First Aid: Guide for Field Workers* (2011); IASC — *Guidelines on Mental Health and Psychosocial Support in Emergency Settings* (2007).

## Community

- **Groups survive better than individuals.** Get to know your neighbors before the crisis.
- Do a skills survey: who works in health care, who understands electrical work, mechanics, farming, cooking, childcare, radio.
- Short, regular meetings; clear, agreed decisions; written records of agreements.
- **Trade:** salt, seeds, medicines, fuel, batteries, tools, skills (repairs, sewing, health care) and preserved food tend to be very valuable.
- Split heavy tasks (water, firewood, watch duty) into shifts.
- Resolve conflicts early, with mediation by someone neutral.

## Security

- **Discretion:** avoid displaying stockpiles, bright lights at night and food smells carrying far; share with people you trust.
- **Prevention is better than confrontation:** reinforced doors, dogs, thorny hedges, lighting, an organized neighborhood watch.
- Go out in pairs, agree on return times and signals.
- Avoid tense crowds and conflicts; the best defense is almost always to **leave** the dangerous place.
- Keep a **go-bag** ready (water, food for 3 days, first aid kit, documents in a plastic bag, cash, flashlight, radio, clothes, medicines, a copy of this kit on a USB flash drive).

## Documents and money

- Printed and digital copies (encrypted USB flash drive) of documents, medical prescriptions, vaccination card and contacts.
- Some cash in small bills for the first days.
