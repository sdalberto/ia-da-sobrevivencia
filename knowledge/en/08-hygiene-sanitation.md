# Hygiene, sanitation and homemade soap

In disasters, diseases such as diarrhea, cholera, leptospirosis and hepatitis A spread due to lack of sanitation. Hygiene is preventive medicine.

## Handwashing

Wash with soap and water for **at least 20 seconds**: before cooking and eating, after using the latrine, changing diapers, caring for the sick or wounds, and handling animals or garbage.

**Improvised tap (tippy-tap):** a jug hung from a frame, with a hole near the cap, tipped by a rope attached to a wooden pedal. Stepping on the pedal makes the water flow. It saves water and doesn't contaminate the container.

Without soap: wood ash rubbed on the hands and rinsed off also reduces germs (better than water alone).

> Source: WHO — *Guidelines on Hand Hygiene*; CDC — Handwashing in emergencies.

## Latrines and feces management

- Location: **at least 30 m** from any water source (well, river, spring), at a **lower** point than it, and with the bottom at least 1.5 m above the water table.
- **Simple pit latrine:** a hole 1–2 m deep, covered with boards or a slab and a lid. After use, throw in a layer of soil, ash or lime: it reduces odor and flies.
- When it is about 50 cm from full, cover it with soil and dig another.
- **Emergency indoors (no flushing):** a strong bucket with a double garbage bag and a lid; after each use, cover with sawdust, dry soil, ash or lime. Close the bags and bury them far from water.
- **Dry/composting toilet:** separate urine (it can be diluted as fertilizer) and cover feces with sawdust; the compost needs at least 1 year before being used on fruit trees (never on vegetables eaten raw).

> Source: The Sphere Handbook (2018) — excreta management standards; WHO — *Guidelines on Sanitation and Health* (2018).

## Garbage

- Separate organic waste (composting or animal feed) from the rest.
- Burn only paper and wood, away from houses. Burnt plastic releases toxic gases.
- Bury what cannot be burned in a pit at least 30 m from water.
- Accumulated garbage attracts rats (leptospirosis) and mosquitoes.

## Mosquitoes (dengue, malaria, Zika, chikungunya)

- Eliminate standing water: cover containers, turn bottles and tires upside down, put sand in plant-pot saucers.
- Mosquito nets over beds, screens on windows, long clothing at dawn and dusk.
- Repellents with DEET, icaridin (picaridin) or IR3535.

## Floodwater and rats

Don't walk barefoot in floodwater or mud (leptospirosis). Wear boots or plastic bags tied on. Wash and disinfect surfaces that were flooded with a solution of **1 cup (240 mL) of 2–2.5% household bleach in 10 liters of water** (with 6% bleach, 1 cup for about 20 liters). Ventilate well and never mix bleach with ammonia or acids.

> Source: Ministério da Saúde (Brazilian Ministry of Health) — Leptospirose (Leptospirosis); CDC — Cleaning and sanitizing after a flood.

## Personal hygiene with little water

- Bathe with a mug or a damp cloth (face, armpits, groin, feet).
- Short hair makes hygiene easier.
- Brush your teeth daily (baking soda or salt if there is no toothpaste).
- Change into dry socks and underwear: prevents fungal infections and "trench foot".
- Reusable cloth pads or a menstrual cup: boil or wash with soap and dry in the sun.

## Homemade soap with ash (no store-bought lye)

Traditional soap is made with **fat + lye** (an alkaline solution from ash).

### 1. Ash lye

1. Use **white** ash from well-burned hardwood (do not use ash from barbecue charcoal with additives, colored paper or treated wood).
2. Put straw or grass at the bottom of a bucket with holes, fill it with ash and slowly pour water through (preferably rainwater). Collect the brown liquid that drips out the bottom.
3. Pass the liquid through the ash again until it is strong: **a fresh egg or a potato should float** with a part the size of a coin showing, or a chicken feather should start to dissolve.

> ⚠️ Lye is **caustic**: it burns skin and eyes. Use gloves, goggles, and containers of plastic, wood, stainless steel or clay (**never aluminum**). Keep children and animals away. If it splashes on skin or eyes, rinse with plenty of running water for 15–20 minutes.

### 2. Saponification

1. Melt and strain fat (lard, tallow, filtered used cooking oil).
2. Over low heat, gradually add the lye to the fat (approximate starting ratio: 2 parts lye to 1 part fat, by volume), stirring constantly.
3. Cook while stirring for 1–3 hours until it becomes a thick, uniform paste, like porridge ("trace").
4. **Test:** a little cooled sample should not "sting" the tongue or be oily. If it is oily, it needs more lye; if it burns, it needs more fat.
5. The result is a **soft soap** (potassium soap). Store in jars. To harden it, add a handful of salt at the end.
6. Let it cure for 2–4 weeks before using it on skin.

> Source: traditional technique documented in various rural manuals; see also Appropedia — Lye from wood ash. https://www.appropedia.org
