# Shelter and body temperature

The body loses heat much faster when wet and in contact with the ground. Shelter is priority number 1 after first aid when the weather is cold, rainy or extremely hot.

## Choosing the site

- Slightly elevated, flat ground, away from riverbeds (even dry ones), unstable slopes and flood-prone areas.
- Away from dead trees or hanging dry branches.
- Close to water (but at least 60 m from it, so as not to attract insects and animals and not to contaminate the source), firewood and shelter material.
- Protected from the prevailing wind (behind rock, woods, an embankment).
- In hot weather: shade and ventilation; in cold weather: morning sun.

## Ground insulation (the most forgotten)

The ground steals heat by conduction all night long. Make a "bed" of **at least 20–30 cm** of dry leaves, grass, thin pine boughs, cardboard or a sleeping pad. When compressed, it should still be about 10 cm thick.

## Quick shelters

### With a tarp or plastic sheet

- **A-frame:** a rope stretched between two trees, tarp over it, edges fixed to the ground with stakes or stones.
- **Lean-to:** tarp sloping down from a horizontal rope or pole; opening facing a reflector fire.
- Keep a steep pitch so rain runs off.

### Without a tarp — debris hut

1. A long pole (ridgepole) resting on a stump or forked stick at waist height, the other end on the ground.
2. Smaller sticks leaning on both sides, forming "ribs".
3. Cover with **lots** of material: leaves, grass, branches — at least 60 cm thick for insulation and rain.
4. Fill the inside with dry leaves. Go in feet first and plug the entrance with a pile of leaves.
5. It should be only slightly bigger than your body: a small space is warmer.

### Urban

- Choose the smallest room in the house, preferably in the center (fewer exterior walls).
- Seal gaps, cover windows with blankets, cardboard or bubble wrap.
- Tents pitched indoors retain a lot of body heat.
- Sleep together, with several layers of covers.

## Clothing: the layering rule

- **Base:** wool or synthetic (wet cotton loses almost all its insulation).
- **Mid:** fleece, wool, down — insulation.
- **Outer:** windbreaker / waterproof.
- Head, neck, hands and feet lose a lot of heat: a hat, scarf and dry socks are essential.
- Avoid sweating in the cold: remove layers before exertion.
- Newspaper and cardboard between layers of clothing work as improvised insulation.

## Extreme heat

- Stay in the shade during the hottest hours (10 a.m.–4 p.m.); work early in the morning and late in the afternoon.
- Light, light-colored, loose clothing; cover your head and neck.
- Wetting your clothes and fanning yourself cools you through evaporation.
- Drink water regularly; don't wait until you feel thirsty.
- See the signs of heatstroke in the [First aid](07-first-aid-medicine.md) guide.

## Safe heating

- Stones heated in the fire (do not use wet river stones: they can explode) wrapped in cloth warm the bed.
- A hot water bottle inside the sleeping bag.
- Never use fire, charcoal or a camp stove inside a tent or closed room without ventilation: risk of **carbon monoxide**.

> Sources: US Army ATP 3-50.21 *Survival* (2018), chapter on shelters; Wilderness Medical Society — Clinical Practice Guidelines for the Out-of-Hospital Evaluation and Treatment of Accidental Hypothermia (2019).
