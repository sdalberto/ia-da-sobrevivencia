# Survival priorities

This guide is the starting point. When the internet, power and public services stop, the order in which you act matters more than any piece of equipment.

> ⚠️ **Warning:** this material is educational and does not replace health professionals, firefighters or civil defense. Whenever help is available, seek it (in Brazil: SAMU ambulance 192, Fire Department 193, Civil Defense 199; elsewhere: 911 in the US/Canada, 112 in Europe).

## The rule of 3

An average person, under normal conditions, can usually survive approximately:

- **3 minutes** without air (or with uncontrolled severe bleeding);
- **3 hours** without shelter in extreme weather (cold with rain, intense heat);
- **3 days** without water;
- **3 weeks** without food.

The numbers are approximate and serve to set **priorities**: first safety and first aid, then shelter/body temperature, then water, then food.

> Source: teaching rule used in survival manuals (e.g. US Army Survival Manual FM 21-76 / ATP 3-50.21).

## The first 72 hours

1. **Immediate safety:** leave hazardous areas (fire, flood, collapse, gas). Shut off the gas and the main power switch if there is a leak or water in the wiring.
2. **Injured people:** control severe bleeding, check breathing (see [First aid](07-first-aid-medicine.md)).
3. **Communication:** agree with your family on a meeting point and a fixed time to try to make contact by radio/phone.
4. **Body temperature:** dry clothes, shelter, insulation from the ground.
5. **Water:** store all the clean water you can (tanks, bathtubs, bottles) before the mains pressure runs out. Plan on at least 3 liters per person per day just for drinking.
6. **Inventory:** list food, medicines, fuel, batteries, tools and who needs special care (babies, elderly people, the chronically ill, pets).
7. **Information:** a battery- or crank-powered AM/FM radio is the most reliable official source when the internet goes down.

## From the first week to the first month

- Establish a routine: shifts for watch, cooking, water collection, hygiene.
- Ration perishable food first (fridge/freezer), then non-perishables.
- Organize sanitation: a latrine site away from water (see [Hygiene](08-hygiene-sanitation.md)).
- Start thinking about production: fast-growing vegetable garden, rainwater harvesting.
- Get closer to your neighbors. Communities survive better than isolated individuals.

## Thinking about 1 year or more

For long periods, survival depends on **producing**, not just stockpiling:

- **Water:** rainwater harvesting + continuous purification.
- **Food:** vegetable garden, non-hybrid seeds, small livestock, food preservation.
- **Health:** prevention (hygiene, safe water, vaccinations up to date before the crisis), stock of medicines for continuous use.
- **Energy:** solar, managed firewood, oil for lamps.
- **Knowledge:** this repository, printed books and offline databases (Kiwix).
- **Skills and trade:** people who can repair, plant, sew, care for the injured and teach are valuable in any community.

## How to use this kit

- Browse the **index** in the web interface or ask the local AI.
- Print the most critical guides (water, first aid, fire) — paper doesn't need power.
- Practice **before** the crisis: making fire, purifying water, CPR and tourniquets are learned with your hands.
