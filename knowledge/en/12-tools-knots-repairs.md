# Tools, knots and repairs

## Essential tools

- **Cutting:** fixed-blade knife, multi-tool pocketknife, hatchet, pruning saw, machete, hoe.
- **Building:** hammer, assorted nails and screws, flathead and Phillips screwdrivers, pliers, handsaw, hand drill (brace), tape measure, level.
- **Lashing:** nylon rope/paracord (30 m or more), annealed wire, nylon cable ties (zip ties), duct tape, epoxy glue.
- **Sewing:** thick and fine needles, strong thread, buttons, leather awl.
- **Sharpening:** whetstone (fine and coarse) or file.
- **Garden:** shovel, hoe, rake, watering can, hose, seeds.
- **Off-grid kitchen:** cast iron pot, pressure cooker, hand grain mill, manual can opener.

Keep tools clean, dry and lightly oiled against rust.

## How to sharpen a knife

1. Wet the stone (water or oil, depending on the type).
2. Keep a constant angle of about **15–20°** between the blade and the stone.
3. Draw the blade as if slicing a thin layer off the stone, from heel to tip, alternating sides (same number of strokes).
4. Start on the coarse side until you feel a small burr on the edge, then finish on the fine side.
5. Finish on leather or cardboard (stropping).

## Essential knots

| Knot | What it's for | How to tie it (summary) |
|---|---|---|
| **Bowline** | A fixed loop that doesn't slip or tighten (rescue, tying around the waist) | Make a small loop in the standing part; the end comes up through the loop, goes around behind the standing part and goes back down through the same loop. "The rabbit comes out of the hole, goes around the tree and goes back down the hole." |
| **Reef knot (square knot)** | Joining two ends of the same rope (bandages, bundles) | Right over left and under; left over right and under. Do not use for important loads. |
| **Sheet bend** | Joining two ropes of different thicknesses | Make a bight in the thick rope; the thin one comes up through the bight, goes around both legs of the bight and passes under itself. |
| **Clove hitch** | Securing a rope to a post or pole (starting lashings) | Two crossed turns around the post, with the end passing under the second turn. |
| **Round turn & two half hitches** | Securing a rope to a tree or ring under load | Two full turns around the anchor and two half hitches around the standing part. |
| **Taut-line hitch** | Tensioning and adjusting tarp/tent guylines | Two turns around the standing part, inside the loop, and one turn outside; it slides to tension and locks under load. |
| **Figure-eight knot** | Stopper knot and secure loop (climbing) | Form an "8" with the end and pass it through the loop. |
| **Prusik** | Friction hitch that slides up another rope and locks under load | A loop of cord making 3 turns around the main rope and passing through itself. |
| **Square lashing** | Joining two poles in a cross (shelters, platforms, stretchers) | Clove hitch on one pole, 3–4 "square" wraps around both poles, 2–3 frapping turns between them, and finish with a clove hitch. |

> Source: *The Ashley Book of Knots* (C. W. Ashley, 1944); US Army ATP 3-50.21 *Survival* (2018), knots appendix; scouting manuals (Escoteiros do Brasil — Scouts of Brazil).

## Improvised cordage

Fibers from embira (inner bark), sisal, banana plant (dried pseudostem fiber), coconut palm fronds and vines can be twisted into cord: twist two strands in the same direction and wrap them around each other in the opposite direction (reverse wrap). Plastic bags cut into strips also make strong cord.

## Common repairs

- **Leaking roof:** a tarp on top held down with battens; sealing compound; replace broken tiles.
- **Leaking pipe:** self-fusing tape or inner tube rubber wrapped tightly and secured with wire/a hose clamp; close the valve first.
- **Clothes and shoes:** sew with strong doubled thread; leather and canvas patches; contact cement.
- **Bicycle:** the most efficient means of transport without fuel. Keep inner tubes, a patch kit, a pump, chain oil and wrenches.
- **Pots with holes:** a copper rivet or a bolt with a rubber washer (only for water pots, away from direct flame).

## Learn and document

Write down on paper everything you learn (recipes, maps, contacts, repairs). Offline libraries such as iFixit (available on Kiwix) teach how to repair thousands of devices.
