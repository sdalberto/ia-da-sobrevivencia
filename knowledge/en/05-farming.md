# Gardening and livestock to survive a year or more

Stockpiles run out. For long periods you need to produce food. Start small, with fast and hardy crops, and expand gradually.

## Principles

- **Sun:** most vegetables need 4–6 hours of direct sun per day.
- **Water:** close to the source, so you can water without waste (water early or late in the afternoon; use mulch to retain moisture).
- **Soil:** loose and rich in organic matter. Compost and aged manure solve almost everything.
- **Diversity:** plant several crops at staggered dates (every 2–3 weeks) for a continuous harvest and lower pest risk.

## Recommended crops (tropical/subtropical climate)

| Crop | Approximate time to harvest | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Radish | 25–35 days | Fastest of all |
| Lettuce, arugula | 40–60 days | Prefers mild weather |
| Collard greens (couve) | 60–80 days, harvest for months | Pick the lower leaves |
| Cowpea / common bean | 60–90 days | Protein, fixes nitrogen in the soil |
| Zucchini / squash | 50–120 days | High yield, stores well (ripe squash) |
| Corn | 90–120 days | Plant in blocks (wind pollination) |
| Sweet potato | 120–150 days | Hardy, the leaves are also edible |
| Cassava (manioc) | 8–18 months | Very hardy, a "living stockpile" in the soil |
| Banana | ~12 months | Perennial, produces all year |
| Ora-pro-nóbis (*Pereskia aculeata*), taioba (*Xanthosoma* spp.), moringa (*Moringa oleifera*) | continuous | Leaves rich in protein and minerals (taioba always cooked) |

In cold or temperate climates prefer: potato, cabbage, carrot, beet, pea, wheat, oats, onion.

## Seeds: the most strategic resource

- Prefer **heirloom or open-pollinated seeds** (not "F1" hybrids): from them you can save seeds that reproduce the same plant.
- Store dry seeds in a tightly closed glass jar, with a little raw rice or silica gel to absorb moisture, in a cool, dark place. Label with name and date.
- Let the best plants "go to seed": the fruits must ripen completely on the plant (squash, tomato, beans drying in the pod).
- Sweet potato, cassava, potato and sugarcane are replanted from vine cuttings, stem cuttings or tubers.

## Fertilizing with what you have

- **Composting:** layers of green material (vegetable food scraps, grass) and brown material (dry leaves, straw, cardboard) in a ratio of about 1:2–3; keep it as damp as a wrung-out sponge and turn it every 1–2 weeks. Ready in 2–4 months.
- **Manure** from chickens, rabbits, horses and cows: let it age before use.
- **Wood ash:** a source of potassium and corrects acidity — use sparingly.
- **Diluted urine** (1 part to 8–10 parts water) is a nitrogen-rich fertilizer; apply it to the soil, not to the leaves of greens that will be eaten raw.
- **Legumes** (beans, pigeon pea *Cajanus cajan*, sunn hemp *Crotalaria*) enrich the soil.

## Pest control without poison

- Remove caterpillars and snails by hand.
- Diluted tobacco, garlic or chili pepper spray repels many insects.
- Companion plants: basil, marigold (*Tagetes*) and cilantro attract natural enemies.
- Crop rotation: don't plant the same family in the same spot repeatedly.

## Small livestock

- **Chickens:** daily eggs, meat, insect control and manure. 3–5 hens supply a family with eggs. They need shelter, clean water and protection from predators. They eat scraps, corn and grass.
- **Rabbits:** reproduce quickly, eat grass and leaves; lean meat (supplement the diet with fat).
- **Fish in ponds (tilapia):** high production in little space where water is available.
- **Bees (native stingless or *Apis*):** honey, wax and pollination.

Meat from livestock must be cooked well. Slaughter away from the water source and bury remains at least 30 m from water.

## Water for the garden

- Improvised drip irrigation: a PET bottle with a small hole buried next to the plant.
- Mulch (straw, leaves) cuts evaporation in half.
- Reuse dishwater (without much soap and without grease) on plants that won't be eaten raw.
