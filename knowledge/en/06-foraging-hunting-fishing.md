# Foraging, fishing and hunting

> ⚠️ Respect local fishing, hunting and environmental protection laws whenever they are in force. When life is at risk, prioritize lower-risk food sources (cultivated plants, fish, insects).

## Wild plants: the main rule

**Only eat what you have identified with 100% certainty.** Many edible plants have poisonous look-alikes. Learn locally, from people who know, before the crisis, and keep a printed illustrated guide for your region.

### Common in Brazil and easy to identify (PANC — "unconventional food plants")

- **Ora-pro-nóbis / Barbados gooseberry** (*Pereskia aculeata*): very high-protein leaves; raw or sautéed.
- **Taioba / arrowleaf elephant ear** (*Xanthosoma*): leaves **always cooked** (raw, it irritates the mouth). Caution: it is confused with toxic plants such as dumb cane (*Dieffenbachia*, "comigo-ninguém-pode") and wild taro ("inhame-bravo"); if in doubt, don't eat it.
- **Beldroega / purslane** (*Portulaca oleracea*): low-growing, fleshy leaves; raw or cooked.
- **Serralha / sow thistle** (*Sonchus oleraceus*) and **dandelion:** bitter leaves, raw or sautéed.
- **Caruru / amaranth** (*Amaranthus*): cooked leaves and seeds.
- **Bertalha / Malabar spinach (*Basella alba*), capuchinha / nasturtium (*Tropaeolum majus*), peixinho-da-horta / lamb's ear (*Stachys byzantina*), palma / prickly pear cactus (*Opuntia*, without spines, cooked).**
- **Fruits:** guava, pitanga / Surinam cherry (*Eugenia uniflora*), jabuticaba (*Plinia cauliflora*), banana, mango, coconut, araçá / strawberry guava (*Psidium cattleianum*), passion fruit, cashew (the cashew apple; the raw nut is caustic — only roasted, with care).

> Source: Kinupp, V. F. & Lorenzi, H. — *Plantas Alimentícias Não Convencionais (PANC) no Brasil* (Unconventional Food Plants in Brazil) (Instituto Plantarum, 2014).

### Warning signs (in general, avoid)

Milky sap (with known exceptions), bitter almond smell, leaves in groups of three, seeds inside unknown pods, plants resembling parsley or wild carrot (hemlock family), unknown white or yellow fruits.

### Mushrooms

**Do not eat wild mushrooms** without identification by an expert. Some of the deadliest (e.g. *Amanita*) look ordinary and only cause symptoms once the liver is already damaged.

### Edibility test

Military manuals describe a "universal edibility test" (testing contact with the skin, lip, tongue and small doses over hours). It is **not reliable for highly toxic plants** and should be a **last resort**, never used for mushrooms.

> Source: US Army ATP 3-50.21 *Survival* (2018), section on plants and the universal edibility test.

## Insects

An abundant source of protein. Termites, beetle larvae (palm weevil grubs, "coró de coqueiro"), grasshoppers and crickets (without legs and wings). **Always cook or roast them** (parasites). Avoid brightly colored, hairy or strong-smelling insects, and caterpillars in general.

## Fishing

- **Line and hook:** improvised hooks from pins, thorns, bone or tin; bait of worms, insects, pieces of fish.
- **Set lines:** several lines tied to branches on the bank overnight — you fish while you sleep.
- **Bottle trap:** a large PET bottle cut near the neck, with the neck inverted and fitted inward, with bait. Small fish get in and can't get out.
- **Stone fish weir** in shallow rivers that funnels fish into a dead-end pool.
- Freshwater fish: **always well cooked** (parasites). Fish with a strong ammonia smell, sunken eyes and gray gills is spoiled.

## Hunting and traps

Traps work 24 hours a day and use less energy than active hunting. Use them only where legal or in a real emergency, and check them daily to avoid animal suffering.

- Place them on animal trails (tracks, droppings, passages under fences), at watering points and at burrow entrances.
- Simple types: a fine-wire snare on a trail, a box trap with a "figure-4" trigger for small animals.
- Birds, wild rodents, cavies (preás), armadillos and fish are usually the most accessible sources.
- **Do not eat animals found dead** (unknown cause) or sick animals.

## Preparing meat safely

- Bleed and gut immediately after killing, without puncturing the intestines and bladder.
- Wear gloves or protect wounds on your hands (diseases such as tularemia, leptospirosis, rabies).
- Cook all game meat well (internal temperature of at least 71–74 °C, no pink parts).
- Liver with white/yellowish spots: discard the animal.
- Very lean rabbits and cavies: supplement the diet with fat (extremely lean meat as the only food causes "rabbit starvation").

> Sources: US Army ATP 3-50.21 *Survival* (2018); CDC — Tularemia and Leptospirosis fact sheets.
