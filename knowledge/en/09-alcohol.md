# Alcohol: fermentation, distillation and uses (antiseptic and fuel)

Ethyl alcohol (ethanol) serves as an **antiseptic**, **fuel** for lamps and stoves, a **solvent** for extracts and a **preservative**. It can be produced from any sugar or starch.

> ⚠️ **Legality:** in many countries home distillation is prohibited or requires a license/registration (in Brazil, commercial production of distilled spirits requires registration with the Ministry of Agriculture; in the US, distilling without a federal license is illegal). Check local law. In a real emergency, follow the safety rules below.
>
> ⚠️ **Safety:** alcohol vapor is **highly flammable**. Poorly done distillation can cause fire and explosion. Never distill indoors, never use an open flame near the distillate outlet, and never drink alcohol of unknown origin or purity.

## 1. Fermentation (making the "mash" or "wash")

Yeast turns sugar into alcohol and carbon dioxide.

### Ingredients (example for ~20 L)

- 4 kg of sugar (or sugarcane juice, rapadura/unrefined cane sugar, diluted honey, mashed ripe fruit)
- Drinking water to make up 20 L
- 1 packet (~10 g) of dry baker's yeast, or cachaça/wine yeast; or wild yeast/mashed ripe fruit (slower and unpredictable)
- Optional: juice of 1 lemon (acidity helps the yeast) and a handful of beans or bran (nutrients)

**Rule of thumb:** about **17 g of sugar per liter yields 1% alcohol**. 200 g/L → ~11–12% alcohol. More than 250 g/L usually stalls fermentation with baker's yeast.

### Steps

1. Dissolve the sugar in part of the water, warm; top up with cold water. The temperature should be between **25 and 30 °C** before adding the yeast (heat above 35 °C kills the yeast).
2. Put it in a food-grade bucket or drum, **filled to 3/4**.
3. Close it with an **airlock**: a hose coming out of the lid with its end submerged in a glass of water. Gas gets out and air doesn't get in (prevents it from turning into vinegar).
4. Leave in a dark place, between 20 and 30 °C, for **7 to 14 days**, until it stops bubbling and the liquid clears.
5. Let it settle and transfer only the clear liquid (without the sediment at the bottom).

### From starch (corn, cassava, potato, rice)

The starch must first be turned into sugar:
- **Malt:** germinated and dried grains (corn, barley) contain enzymes. Cook the starch in water, let it cool to 63–68 °C, add the ground malt (about 20–30% of the weight) and hold at that temperature for 1–2 hours. The porridge becomes sweet and thin. Then cool and ferment.
- The Indigenous Brazilian tradition of cauim uses saliva (enzymes) for this — not recommended for hygiene reasons.

> Source: fermentation principles described in FAO — *Fermented fruits and vegetables: a global perspective* (1998); Appropedia — Ethanol fuel production.

## 2. Distillation

Alcohol boils at **78 °C**, water at 100 °C. By heating the mash slowly, the vapor that comes off first is rich in alcohol; when it condenses, it becomes distillate.

### Minimum equipment

- **Pot or boiler** of stainless steel or copper with a sealed lid (use a flour-and-water paste as a seal).
- **Outlet tube** of copper or stainless steel (never plastic, lead, lead solder or zinc/galvanized — they contaminate).
- **Coil/condenser:** the tube passes through a bucket of cold water (change the water when it gets warm).
- **Thermometer** (ideal) and numbered glass containers for collecting.
- A controllable **heat source**, preferably **electric**, or with the flame well away from and shielded from the distillate outlet.

### Separating the fractions (very important)

1. **Heads (discard):** the first ~**50 mL for every 20 L of mash** (or the first 1–2% of the volume). Contains methanol, acetone and acetaldehyde. Smells of solvent/nail polish. **Discard or use only as lamp fuel outdoors.**
2. **Hearts:** the good distillate, with a clean smell. Collect while the vapor temperature is between ~78 and 92 °C.
3. **Tails:** when the smell turns to "wet dog"/oily and the strength drops (vapor above ~95 °C). Set aside and add to the next batch to redistill.

- **Methanol** is toxic (blindness, death). **Sugar and grain** mashes produce little; **fruit mashes with skins and pulp** (pectin) produce more. Even so, always discard the heads.
- **One distillation** in a simple pot still gives about 30–50% alcohol; **a second distillation** of the distillate (diluted to ~30%) reaches 70–85%. Packed columns (stainless steel scrubbers, copper rings) reach ~90–95%.
- Turn off the heat if the flow turns to vapor coming out of the tip (cooling water is hot): alcohol vapor can ignite.

## 3. Measuring and diluting

- An **alcoholmeter** (hydrometer for alcohol, Gay-Lussac scale) is the correct way to measure strength.
- **Improvised fire test:** alcohol above ~50% burns on its own with a blue flame; below that, soaked into a cloth, it does not sustain a flame. This is only a rough indication.
- **Diluting:** use the formula **C1 × V1 = C2 × V2**.
  - Example: to make 1 L of 70% alcohol from 92% alcohol: V1 = 70 × 1000 ÷ 92 ≈ **760 mL of 92% alcohol + boiled water to make up 1 L**.

## 4. Uses

### Antiseptic (skin and surfaces)

- **60–80% alcohol is more effective than 95% alcohol** as a disinfectant (water helps denature the proteins of microorganisms). The standard is **70%**.
- It doesn't work well on dirty surfaces: clean first with soap and water.
- It does not sterilize (does not kill spores). For instruments, prefer boiling for 20 minutes.

### WHO hand-rub formula (for 10 L)

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Ethanol 96% | 8,333 mL |
| Hydrogen peroxide 3% | 417 mL |
| Glycerin (glycerol) 98% | 145 mL |
| Distilled water, or boiled and cooled | to make up 10,000 mL |

Final concentration: ethanol 80%, glycerol 1.45%, peroxide 0.125%. Let it stand for 72 hours before use (eliminates any spores present). For smaller quantities, divide everything by 10 (1 L).

> Source: WHO — *Guide to Local Production: WHO-recommended Handrub Formulations* (2010). https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-IER-PSP-2010.5

### Fuel

- **Lamp:** a glass jar with a perforated metal lid and a cotton wick. Alcohol above ~80% burns cleanly.
- **Can stove (alcohol stove):** two aluminum can bottoms fitted together with holes around the rim; burns alcohol above ~85–90% efficiently. One tablespoon boils a little water.
- An alcohol flame is **almost invisible in daylight**. Never refuel while the stove is lit or hot.

### Other

- **Tinctures:** medicinal herbs left in alcohol (40–60%) for 2–4 weeks extract the active compounds (use only known herbs).
- **Preservative** for fruit and extracts (vanilla).
- **Degreaser** and glass cleaner.

> ⚠️ Never drink alcohol produced for fuel or antiseptic: it may contain methanol, additives or contaminants from the equipment.
